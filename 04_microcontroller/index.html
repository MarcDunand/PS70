This is what my 3D printer Manta board is built for. It even comes with a preset coreXY mode which calculates the kinematics of how to convert the diagonal motion of turning a single motor into orthoganol motion instead.</p> <p>The really interesting parts are the stick and the pumps. The stick simply needs to be able to go up and down; it is the very basic z-axis of our gantry. When it is up, it floats above the surface of the marbling and when it's down it adds patterns into the surface of the marbling. Effectively, it works exactly like a pen plotter. The fundamental controls for the peristaltic pumps remains the same. They need to be able to pump forward, backward, stop, and pulse (now called dose).</p> <p>Since the manta board executes gcode, we needed to create custom gcode commands that corresponded to stick up, stick down, motors forward, motors backward, motors stop, and motors dose. Here is the code that lives on the Manta board which achieves this:</p> <a id="btn" href="./pumps_and_stick.cfg" download>Custom Gcode Commands</a> <p class="margin"> </p> <p>In essence, this gives us a number of new gcode commands which the board will be able to parse: </p> <p>STICK_UP: moves the stick out of the marbling</p> <p>STICK_DOWN: moves the stick into the marbling</p> <p>PUMP_FORWARD PUMP=i, where i is the integer index of the pump</p> <p>PUMP_REVERSE PUMP=i, where i is the integer index of the pump</p> <p>PUMP_STOP PUMP=i, where i is the integer index of the pump</p> <p>DOSE PUMP=i SECONDS=s DIR=d, where i is the integer index of the pump, s is a float for the number of seconds, and d=FWD|REV is the direction for the dose.</p> <p>Now, in order to get these commands into the gcode correctly, I needed to create a second program. To create a file for my paper marbler to run, I design the pattern that I want it to make in inkscape. Then, the SVG that I make in inkscape is converted into gcode which the Manta board is able to interpret. It is in that gcode that I need my custom gcode commands to move the stick and to activate the pumps. To make this happen, I created a custom gcode compiler, which takes an SVG as input and looks for certain features. If it sees a colored circle anywhere in the SVG file, it interprets that as a command to drop ink of that color at that location of the marbling.
